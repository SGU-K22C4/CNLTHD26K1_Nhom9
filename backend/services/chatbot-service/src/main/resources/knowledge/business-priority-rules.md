# Business Priority Rules

## Priority product groups

1. shirts and blouses
2. trousers
3. basic tees
4. blazers
5. wearable dresses and skirts
6. straight or wide jeans

The chatbot should prefer items that are easy to wear, easy to style, and easy to close.

## Hero products

### Women

- white basic shirt
- black straight-leg trousers
- beige or camel oversized blazer
- black A-line midi skirt
- white basic tee
- wrap midi dress
- blue straight jeans
- thin crew-neck knitwear
- striped midi skirt
- beige trench coat
- draped blouse
- gray wide-leg trousers

### Men

- white oxford shirt
- black slim or straight trousers
- white or black basic tee
- navy blazer
- indigo straight jeans
- linen shirt
- light windbreaker
- beige chinos

## When to prefer hero products

- the customer asks generally what is good
- the customer is buying for the first time
- the customer is undecided between many options
- the customer asks for a bestseller or safest choice

Do not force hero products when:

- the customer already wants a very specific item
- the hero item does not match the occasion
- the hero item does not have the right size

## Upsell and cross-sell

- shirt -> trousers or belt
- jeans -> tee or light outerwear
- dress -> blazer or light jacket
- blazer -> inner top

## Easy-to-close styling rules

Safe styles:

- white or black top with trousers or blue jeans
- all-white outfit
- monochrome beige or camel
- oversized blazer with straight trousers

Harder-to-close styles:

- bold graphics
- highly saturated red or neon yellow
- complex cut-outs
- multi-layer dramatic styling

## Objection handling

If the customer says the item is expensive:

1. explain value first
2. explain versatility or cost-per-wear
3. offer a softer alternative in the same style direction

If the customer hesitates:

- ask whether the hesitation is about price, size, or silhouette
- do not end the conversation too early
